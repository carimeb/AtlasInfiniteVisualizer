Atlas Infinite separates compute from storage. Compute nodes run queries and transactions; an independent, multi-AZ storage layer keeps data durable and coordinates failover. Toggle Atlas Core ↔ Atlas Infinite to compare, then press ▶ Simulate Write or ▶ Simulate Read to follow each step.

Step by step
    Click any compute node to make it fail, or click an Availability Zone header to take the whole zone down (compute + its storage replicas). Watch how the storage layer coordinates failover — no election among compute nodes — and how the Log Service majority (2 of 3 AZs) keeps writes durable.
    🛡 One Availability Zone is down — the cluster keeps serving
    The Log Service still holds a majority (2 of 3 zones), so writes keep being committed durably. If the primary lived in the failed zone, the standby in another zone took over.
    ⛔ Storage-layer majority lost — writes cannot be acknowledged
    🔴 No compute node available
    No compute node can serve reads or writes right now. Data stays durable in the storage layer; service resumes as soon as a compute node is available again.

      What the application sees
      Compute and storage scale independently. The cluster tier sets compute (plus storage throughput and IOPS) — storage capacity grows on its own up to 128 TB per replica set. Pick a tier to watch a vertical resize without moving data, then unleash an AI-agent traffic spike on the auto-scaler.
      ✋ Max storage limit reached — writes blocked, reads still served
      The logical data size approached the max storage limit you set. Atlas blocks writes but keeps serving reads. Raise the limit (or delete data) to resume writes — no tier change needed.

      Compute auto-scaling — demand vs provisioned capacity
      Illustrative model for operational nodes (primary + standby). Scale up: >90% for 10 min (20 min on M10/M20) or a 1-hour average >75%. Scale down: CPU averages below 45% in both the 10-min and 4-hour windows, projected memory on the lower tier below 60% in both windows, and at most once per 24 h.
      The simulation uses utilization as a proxy for CPU and memory; WiredTiger cache is assumed below 90%, and recent provisioning/restart guards are not simulated.
      Predictive: M30+, after 2 weeks of history, up to 2 tiers ahead of cyclical peaks; never scales down. The minimum must also be M30+ when down-scaling is enabled, as it is here. Scaling is rolling, with no downtime, within the min/max tiers and tier class. For planned bursts such as bulk inserts, scale up ahead of time. Capacity units and demand curves are illustrative.
